# Studio photography setup prompt

Paste this block after the car-placement instructions in any Higgsfield blend prompt (image 1 = studio, image 2 = car). Use it in place of the older "Shot on a Sony A7 III, 50mm, f/4" line.

## Car-placement part (change the view per photo)

Place the car from image 2 into the empty studio in image 1. Keep the studio exactly as it is: navy walls, orange neon strips, ceiling softbox, glossy navy floor. Cut the car out completely and stand it on the centre of the studio floor in the same [front three-quarter / side profile / rear three-quarter / straight rear] view it was photographed, facing the same direction as in the photo (do not mirror it). Do not change the car itself: same paint, lights, badges, wheels and number plate, and keep every existing mark, scuff, sticker and sign of wear. No retouching, nothing added or removed.

## Photography setup

CAMERA POSITION
- Full-frame professional camera, height 1.35–1.45 m from the floor, about 6–8 m from the vehicle
- 50mm full-frame equivalent lens, f/8, ISO 100, 1/125 sec, white balance 5000–5500K
- Focus precisely on the vehicle; natural, realistic perspective; vertical lines corrected; minimal barrel distortion; no ultra-wide distortion
- Camera at the vehicle's upper-door / window-line level, aimed level, for a natural showroom perspective

VEHICLE COMPOSITION
- Vehicle centred slightly below the vertical centre, entire vehicle visible, all four wheels visible where appropriate
- Small breathing room around the vehicle, enough floor visible underneath
- No cropping of mirrors, bumpers, roof, wheels or tyres
- Car occupies approximately 65–75% of the horizontal frame, realistic proportions

LIGHTING
- Large soft overhead diffused lights, large soft frontal fill, soft side lighting, controlled rear/rim illumination
- Gentle highlights along the body panels, soft natural shadow underneath, subtle realistic floor reflection, even exposure across the vehicle
- Reveal paint colour accurately, body lines, panel gaps, headlights, wheels, tyres, glass, grille and exterior trim
- Avoid dramatic lighting that hides vehicle details

PHOTOGRAPHIC STYLE
High-end professional car dealership listing photograph, expensive full-frame DSLR/mirrorless camera. Photorealistic, natural dynamic range, accurate paint colour, realistic metallic reflections, tyre texture, glass reflections and shadows, subtle depth of field, sharp vehicle details, clean but believable environment. No excessive HDR, no oversharpening, no CGI look, no exaggerated reflections, no excessive red glow, no cinematic colour grading, no lens flare, no fake bokeh.

COMPOSITING
Fixed camera position, level horizon, consistent floor perspective, physically believable lighting, realistic floor contact lighting. No other vehicle, no people, no text, no logos. The car must not look distorted or pasted onto the background.

FINAL LOOK
Premium Australian car dealership + professional automotive photographer + realistic used-car listing, not a movie poster: clean, trustworthy, premium, realistic, professional, ready for sale.
