# "Queue Jump": GTA VI-style plan (Arfan, 22 Nov 2026)

> **FILM, NOT HDR (Arfan, 22 Nov 2026, overrides grade notes):** every shot must look like A-grade cinema camera footage: ARRI Alexa 35 + anamorphic lenses, soft highlight roll-off, natural (not crunchy) contrast, true skin tones, shadows allowed to fall into real darkness, gentle film grain, mild halation only on practical lights. NEVER: HDR look, local-contrast pop, over-sharpened detail, glowing edges, everything-saturated frames, lifted flat shadows. Every image and video prompt ends with: "Shot on ARRI Alexa 35 with anamorphic lenses, natural film contrast, soft highlight roll-off, true skin tones, fine film grain. No HDR look, no over-sharpening, no oversaturation." The GTA VI mood comes from palette and light direction, not from processing.


Goal: every scene feels like the GTA VI trailers' world, set in Perth, filmed as real live action. Real cars, real fuel numbers, real Perth fuel price. No GTA/Rockstar names, logos, fonts, HUD or music.

## 1. Real numbers (being researched now → `roles/REAL-NUMBERS-v2.md`)
- Montage + Dave: real petrol cars at the C-HR's price (about $25–30k used in Perth) with **real-world 9–11 L/100km** from Australian road tests (Drive, CarExpert, CarsGuide, WhichCar, RACV).
- Our C-HR Hybrid: real-world **5.2 L/100km** (RACV).
- Claim: real-world vs real-world only, e.g. "Half the fuel of his [car].*", with the source line. If the two tests aren't comparable, the line gets softened rather than overstated.
- Pump prices: today's real Perth ULP price from FuelWatch; Dave's and Arfan's totals worked out from real tank sizes.

## 2. The GTA VI look (grade)
- **Golden hour / day:** saturated warm sun, pink-orange sky gradients, teal shadows, high contrast, soft halation on highlights, palm silhouettes, heat haze.
- **Night:** wet roads, sodium-orange + LED-white streetlights, practical neon from real signs (pink, teal, red) reflecting on cars and puddles, deep blacks.
- **Camera:** anamorphic feel (horizontal flare streaks, oval bokeh), fine film grain, low hero angles, slow push-ins, car-mount tracking, one drone-style aerial.
- One grade (LUT) applied to every shot in the edit, so it's one world.

## 3. GTA VI editing style (all free, done in the edit)
- **Location card** at the top: bold `PERTH.` over the opening shot (our Montserrat font).
- **Locals montage** cut hard to the music beat, like the trailers' "people of Vice City".
- **Phone-video inserts:** one shaky vertical "someone filmed this" clip (a bystander filming the V8 launch), framed as a phone clip with no real app UI.
- **Freeze-frame character intros** (the classic GTA move): a 6–8 frame freeze with a name in our font:
  - `DAVE` (at the pump);
  - `ARFAN` (stepping out of the C-HR; name only, no title, so it isn't a cringe "meet our CEO");
  - `MONSOOR` (stepping out of the muscle car).
- **"BUSTED."** card on the speed-camera flash (our font, white).
- **Music-driven cuts:** cuts land on beats; one music dropout for the silent C-HR gag.
- **Trailer-style end:** the logo over a golden-hour hero shot, then the phone number.

## 5. Sound
- Every cut has its own designed sound: indicator, low-fuel chime, gum pop, V8 roar, camera strobe, pump ticks, door clunk, magpie, muscle-car rumble, keys.
- Risers into each freeze-frame; a hard music stop for the silence gag; a cricket.
- Mix: dialogue first, music −13 dB under lines, −14 LUFS, −1 dBTP.

## 6. Music — honest answer
We **can't** use the GTA VI trailer songs or Rockstar's music. They're licensed commercial tracks, Meta's rights system auto-mutes or blocks ads that use them, and it's a copyright claim risk. Instead: an **original track** (ElevenLabs Music, about 11 credits) written for the same feeling: a warm, driving 80s-tinged groove that builds, drops out for the silence gag, and lands on the logo. It's in the spirit of the trailers, but no imitation of any real song.
