## (c) Hook audit: will the red-light montage stop the scroll?

**Verdict: yes, with three fixes.** A rapid montage of Perth faces is a good thumb-stop: people, motion and a relatable pain in a vertical frame all help (Emplifi Facebook Reels data: a person on screen early +3.6%, vertical +9.1% ([Emplifi](https://emplifi.io/resources/facebook-reels-data/))). Its weakness is that it has **no human voice** in the first 3 s. Speech starting in the first 3 s lifted 3-second retention by about 11.7% over music-only videos (same source; vendor data).

Fixes:
1. **Add one human sound by 1.5 s**: a driver's tired "Come on..." or a sigh-and-tap on the gauge. One short new line, the rest diegetic.
2. **One readable word on screen from frame 1**, held while the pictures cut underneath. Fast cuts with no anchor read as noise.
3. **No phone in any driver's hand.** In WA, holding a phone while driving is illegal even when stopped at lights ([Road Safety Commission](https://www.wa.gov.au/organisation/road-safety-commission/mobile-phones-and-distractions)). The servo queue's "faces lit by phones" (EMOTION-AND-SOUND §1, 2a) needs drivers parked and out of gear, or no phones at all. Ad Standards applies the FCAI code to unsafe-driving depictions ([FCAI code](https://adstandards.com.au/sites/default/files/fcai_voluntary_advertising_code_of_practice.pdf)).

**Exact 0–3 s on-screen text (Montserrat ExtraBold, warm white, soft navy #0F2747 shadow, centred, inside the safe area):**

| Time | Picture (cuts every 0.4–0.7 s) | Text on screen (held across cuts) |
|---|---|---|
| 0.0–1.3 | Driver 1 at a red light glances at the gauge (needle near E), FX: indicator tick. Driver 2 taps the trip computer, FX: tap + groan. Driver 3, a tradie, FX: ute idle rattle. | **"Perth. Every red light."** |
| 1.3–2.4 | Driver 4: eyebrows up at the dash, FX: "Come on..." Driver 5, a mum, FX: kids in the back. | **"Every red light."** stays; under it: **"Same look."** |
| 2.4–3.4 | The last driver is Dave: his 2021 ASX dash at the red light, with a graphic showing **7.6 L/100km** (labelled official, not a fake trip reading; section h) | **"7.6 L/100km."** (big number, about 120 px), small line: "his ASX, official*" |

**Number treatment:** only one real number per beat, and every number shown must match the disclaimer. The montage trip computers show **no readable numbers** (soft focus, or the needle only), so no unbadged car makes a hidden claim. The hero number is Dave's **7.6**. At the claim beat it ticks down to **4.3** on the C-HR, with the claim line. Big numbers stay on screen for at least 1.2 s.

**Sound-off proofing:** Meta says over 75% of Instagram Reels views are sound-on ([Meta for Developers](https://developers.facebook.com/blog/post/2024/11/07/unlock-the-power-of-reel-ads/)), so build for sound first. But burn in captions anyway: most Facebook feed views and many Reels views start muted, and vendor guides say captions are a must ([Metricool](https://metricool.com/facebook-reels-ads-guide/)).
- Burn in every spoken line as a caption (Mia, Arfan, Monsoor, the voice-over), not just the supers. Speaker-coloured captions: aqua for Arfan, warm white for others.
- Four **story-spine supers** carry the plot muted: "Perth. Every red light." → "7.6 L/100km." → "Over 40% less fuel than his ASX.*" → "You choose. We deliver." Someone who reads only these four still gets the story.
- The silence gag (8.0 s) shows up muted too: every head turns at once, and the caption reads "*(silence)*".
- **Safe zone:** keep text and faces out of the top ~14% and bottom ~35% (about 250 px top / 400 px bottom at 1080×1920, matching our house rule). Turn on the **Safe zone guardrail** in Ads Manager preview ([Cropink](https://cropink.com/reels-ads); [Metricool](https://metricool.com/facebook-reels-ads-guide/)).

## (f) What the research says to change (top 5, ranked)

1. **Replace the claim (blocker).** "Over 40% less fuel than his ASX.\*" on official figures, with Dave's dash showing 7.6 ticking to the C-HR's 4.3 (section e, item 1). It is honest, still dramatic, and every number on screen matches the disclaimer.
2. **Fast cuts, slow text.** The client's 1–2 s shot pace fits Reels. Native, fast creative helps recall (Google/Arla: ad recall lift rose from 3% to 9% with native Shorts ([Think with Google](https://business.google.com/us/think/search-and-video/shorts-winning-formula-arla-foods/))). But there is **no independent study fixing an ideal cut rate** ([search notes](https://www.influencers-time.com/editing-style-testing-the-pacing-brief-boosting-watch-time/); unverified figures). So: cut pictures every 0.8–2 s, but **hold each story super for 1.2–2 s across several cuts**, and caption every line. The plot survives sound-off because the words stay still while the pictures move. Keep the deliberate 2.5 s silence hold: that one long shot is what makes the fast ones land.
3. **A human voice in the first 1.5 s** (a driver's "Come on..." in Variant A; "Hello mates" cold open in Variant B). Speech in the first 3 s lifted 3-second retention by about 11.7% ([Emplifi](https://emplifi.io/resources/facebook-reels-data/), vendor data). A/B the two openings; that is the main test.
4. **A laugh or smile beat about every 5–6 s, running through picture, sound and edit.** System1 finds amusement is among the strongest brand-building emotions, and humour adapts well to very short formats ([System1](https://system1group.com/blog/humour-in-advertising)). A Twente experiment found humorous, shorter online video ads raised purchase intention ([de Kroon](https://essay.utwente.nl/72909/1/DeKroon_MA_BMS.pdf)). **No study sets a laugh frequency**, so the 5–6 s rhythm is our editorial judgement. Per the client lock, **no inserted gags** (so no cricket, no cartoon "ding", no smash-cut joke). Every laugh must come from the situation. Our situational beats, strongest first: ① Mia's "Dad... can we get one?" (deadpan, then Dave's "don't start" face that cracks); ② the servo going quiet as the C-HR rolls in (real silence: the music drops because the scene does); ③ **Arfan's pump stops early** while Dave's is still running: same pump, fewer litres. That is the honest version of "tops up fast" (section i, point 3); ④ Dave's double take at the silent start; ⑤ the montage faces (a tap on the gauge, a groan); ⑥ Monsoor's dry "Good job." These are 5–6 beats in 28 s, about one every 5 s, without one added joke.
5. **Brand earlier, not only at the end.** A 1 s end card is too short to read. Put the Monsoor dealer plate frame on the C-HR from its first frame (already planned), the yard sign in the first yard shot, and the logo watermark top-left at 55% from 0 s. Keep the end card to logo + phone + MD32748 + *source line, and test a 1.5 s card in Variant C.

Also: trim "That means a lot for me" to under 1.5 s in the paid cuts (keep it whole in organic). Note that the caption must match the audio exactly.

## (h) Dash close-up, odometer, silent start (and the cut shipment sequence)

**Update:** the shipment/port sequence is **cut** from the locked spine. The import rules below stay on file in case "imported by us" ever appears in copy. The dash close-up (now at the servo) and the silent start are still in, so the odometer and L/100km rules apply in full.

**Verdict: ⚠️ pass with changes.** Real numbers on a real car are the strongest proof we have, and the most dangerous thing to fake. Used-car misdescription is an ACCC focus: over 14,000 second-hand-car reports in 2024–25 ([ACCC report, Dec 2025](https://www.accc.gov.au/system/files/consumer-issues-second-hand-cars-dec-2025.pdf)); the ACCC's case against Grays ended in a $10 m penalty for misleading car descriptions ([ACCC](https://www.accc.gov.au/media-release/grays-to-redress-consumers-and-pay-10-million-in-penalties-for-misleading-descriptions-of-cars-for-sale-by-auction)). Odometer winding on Japanese imports is a known issue ([CHOICE](https://www.choice.com.au/transport/cars/used/articles/odometer-tampering-fraud); [WA Consumer Protection](https://consumerprotection.wa.gov.au/odometer-tampering)).

**Odometer: show real kilometres honestly**
1. Use **one real stock car** (our black C-HR Hybrid). Shoot or photograph its actual odometer on the day; that reading must **match the listing** and the Japanese **auction sheet** we keep (the auction report is already a truth check in REEL-PLAN.md).
2. **Composite the real photo** into the dash close-up. Never let the AI generate odometer digits. A generated "38,214 km" is an invented fact about a car we sell.
3. Small super on the shot: "Actual odometer, stock #[ID], [date]". If the car sells before the ad ends, **pull or re-edit the ad**, or switch to rule 4.
4. If no single real car is used, **blur the odometer** or show no digits, and label the shot "Illustrative". Don't show plausible fake numbers.

**Dash L/100km**
- A trip-computer reading reflects one drive, not the car's rating. Showing "4.3" on the dash beside our claim is fine **only if** it is (a) the real trip reading from that car on a stated drive, with a super like "Trip reading, 22 km city loop, [date]", or (b) clearly a graphic of the **official figure** (label: "Official combined 4.3 L/100km*") rather than a fake dash display.
- Never show a dash figure lower than our cited source, and don't show the ASX's "7.6" as Dave's trip reading. Label it "official" (as in section c).

**Shipment and "imported by us"**
- "Imported by us" or "we import" is a factual claim (ACL s18, s29). Say it **only if Monsoor Motors Pty Ltd is the importer of record** for the cars shown. Keep for each car: the bill of lading or shipping papers, the import approval / Register of Approved Vehicles entry under the Road Vehicle Standards Act ([Dept of Infrastructure RVS](https://www.infrastructure.gov.au/infrastructure-transport-vehicles/vehicles/road-vehicle-standards-laws)), the auction sheet, and the export certificate. If we buy imports through an agent or another importer, say "**Japanese import**" (a description of the car), not "imported by us".
- **Shipment imagery:** AI ship or port shots are fine as atmosphere, labelled "Dramatisation". But don't show anything that implies a fact we can't back up: a named shipping line, a "direct from Japan" banner, a specific port, a time ("2 weeks from Japan"), or our logo on a container we don't use.
- **Silent hybrid start:** true for the C-HR Hybrid (it starts in EV mode when conditions allow). Keep it as shown; don't claim "drives silently" or "zero emissions". The ACCC treats "zero emissions" for EVs as misleading, let alone for a hybrid ([CarExpert on ACCC](https://www.carexpert.com.au/car-news/electric-cars-arent-zero-emissions-australian-consumer-watchdog)).
- **Drive-in to our yard:** walking pace, belt on, no phone (FCAI cl.2).

**Edit (locked spine):** dash insert 1–1.2 s right after the top-up; silent start 1 s (push-button, "READY" light, no engine note); Dave's double take 0.8 s; the departure holds the plate frame + door decal readable for at least 0.8 s. The plate frame is also our earliest brand cue (about 10 s).

## (i) Blunt critique of the locked spine (what is still illogical, weak or cringe for Perth)

1. **Illogical for Perth: a servo price board "ticking up" at night.** Under FuelWatch, Perth metro prices are locked for 24 hours from 6 am. They follow a weekly cycle that jumps on Wednesday and is cheapest on **Tuesday** ([WA Consumer Protection](https://consumerprotection.wa.gov.au/fuelwatch-and-fuel-prices)). Every local knows this, so a price board changing at night reads as "made by someone not from here". **Fix:** the only thing ticking is Dave's **pump meter** (litres and dollars, blurred dollar total per rule e-3), and set the night as **Tuesday** (the cheap-day queue is real Perth behaviour). Change the super from "Thursday night" to "Tuesday night. Perth." This is the most local, most shareable truth in the ad. Use it.
2. **Illogical: the keys handover at the yard, then "We bring it to your home!"** If Dave collects the keys at our yard, the delivery CTA contradicts the story we just showed. **Fix:** at the yard Dave **chooses** the car (hand on the bonnet, a nod, a handshake), and Monsoor gives Arfan the "Good job". The **keys are handed over in Dave's driveway** by Arfan. Then the V.O. is a promise we have just seen kept.
3. **Illogical: "tops up fast".** A hybrid doesn't fill faster: the pump flows at the same rate. Viewers who know cars will call it out in the comments. **Fix:** the joke is that his pump **clicks off first** while Dave's is still running (fewer litres to fill). That is true, and funnier because it's the situation.
4. **Weak or rude: the title "Queue Jump".** If Arfan looks like he pushes in, he is the villain in an Aussie servo. Nothing is worse in Perth than a queue jumper. **Fix:** he takes a free pump at the end of the row, or the queue is for the cheap-day car wash or the in-store line. Keep "Queue Jump" as an internal name only; never on screen.
5. **Cringe risk: "Hello mates."** Said by a stranger stepping out of a car to a queue of tired drivers at night, the plural "mates" lands as try-hard or salesman-ish to many Australians. A natural Perth version is a nod and a "G'day" or "How ya going". It is his locked voice track, so the fix is in the **delivery**: say it to **Dave only**, half-turned while unhooking the nozzle, with no pause for reaction. Small and throwaway is likeable; a beat to camera is cringe.
6. **Cringe risk: "Good job" / "That means a lot for me".** This is the closest the ad gets to the banned "meet our new CEO" moment: the company praising itself in front of a customer. Perth audiences punish self-congratulation. It works only if it is **tiny and private**: Monsoor says it under his breath while Dave and Mia are busy with the car, a quick shoulder pat, Arfan's reaction under 1.5 s. Also, "means a lot **for** me" is non-standard English; burned-in captions make it more visible, and comments may mock it. My recommendation: record "That means a lot" alone (it is already in the locked track), or accept it knowingly. Captions must match the audio either way.
7. **Logic gap: whose C-HR does Dave buy?** If Arfan's car carries a door decal and plate frame, viewers may think Dave bought Arfan's own car. **Fix:** at the yard, Dave's car is a second C-HR (a different colour, e.g. white or silver, which also helps viewers follow the story), or the decal is shown being peeled off. One clear second car beats confusion.
8. **Weak: "next day" is fast for a $25–30k decision.** It's fine as story compression, but add the super **"Next day."** so the jump reads as intentional. The ad copy must not imply instant approval or finance (no finance claims at all without credit licence checks).
9. **Brand-fit risk: the GTA VI nod in a dad-and-daughter family story.** A crime-game reference beside a sleeping child is a strange mix, and the "nod" can read as try-hard if it's obvious. Keep it to light and lens only (section g); if a test viewer says "is this GTA?", it has gone too far.
10. **Pace vs. story:** 28 s at 1–2 s shots means about 18–24 AI shots, each a chance for a morphing face or badge, and over budget against the 250-credit cap (REEL-PLAN.md assumed far fewer, longer shots). **Fix:** get the pace from **cutting between takes of the same 4–5 generations**, not from 20 separate generations. The montage can be stock-like close-ups (eyes, gauge, hands) that AI handles well.
11. **The montage drivers watching L/100km at a red light** is a slight stretch: real people glance at the fuel needle, not the trip computer. Use the **needle near E** and the low-fuel warning light (readable sound-off); keep the trip computer for Dave only.

What is genuinely strong and should not be touched: the Tuesday-night servo truth, the silence as the C-HR arrives, Mia's line, the plate frame as "how Dave finds us" (a logical, earned brand reveal), and ending on a promise kept at home.

### The one line (brand card, held for the whole ~5 s)
On the claim beat, the super itself reads: **"Over 40% less fuel than his ASX. Official figures.\*"** (no extra small print there).
On the brand card, one line under the logo and phone, Montserrat Medium, about 30 px, warm white at 80%, centred, above the bottom 400 px:

That is the whole legal footprint. Name = the logo, licence = MD32748, claim source = the rest. It fits on one line at 1080 px wide (about 60 characters). If it wraps, drop "combined" before shrinking the type.
