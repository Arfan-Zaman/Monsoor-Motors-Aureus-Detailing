Researched 21 Nov 2026 for the "Queue Jump" reel. Plan only: nothing generated, nothing committed.

**Brief (client change, 21 Nov):** every montage car is the same type and budget as our C-HR Hybrid. That means a petrol **small SUV** costing about **$25–30k used in Perth**, like Dave's 2021 ASX LS. No utes, no sedans. The ute, 4WD and sedan research from the first pass has been dropped.

## 2. How to compare fairly (official vs official)

Each comparison has to stay on one basis. Pick one row and stick to it in the label and the voiceover.

| Basis | ASX 2021 | Seltos 2022 | CX-3 2024 | Kona 2024 | C-HR Hybrid | Use? |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A. Official ADR 81/02 combined** | 7.6 | 6.8 | 6.3 | 6.6 | 4.3 (AU-market Hybrid) | **Recommended for on-screen labels.** Same lab test for every car |
| B. CarExpert week tests | high 8s | 7.2 | 7.3 | 7.3 | none (RACV 5.2) | Good backup. Mixed outlets for the C-HR |
| C. WhichCar 2020 megatest | 10.4 | 8.0 | 8.7 | — | — | **Don't pair with the C-HR 5.2.** It included drag-strip runs and spirited country driving |

**Saving on basis A:** ASX 7.6 vs C-HR 4.3 = **3.3 L/100km less (about 43%)**. Seltos 2.5 less, CX-3 2.0 less, Kona 2.3 less.

**Legal flag (ACL, misleading claims).** Putting "10.4" (a hard-driven test) next to "5.2" (three gentle weeks) suggests a 50% saving that the same driver wouldn't get. If the label says "real world", either source both figures from like-for-like driving or use basis A and say "official combined". Also, the AU-market ADR 4.3 belongs to the Australian 2020 C-HR Hybrid. A 2018 JDM import was never ADR-rated, so the label should read something like "C-HR Hybrid official combined 4.3 L/100km (Australian-model rating)".

## 3. Notes per car

### 3.1 Dave's 2021 Mitsubishi ASX LS 2.0
- **The 10.4 is verified, but with caveats.** WhichCar's "Which small SUV has the best real-world fuel efficiency?" (published 19 Mar 2020) has the ASX 9th of 10 at **10.4 L/100km** against a **7.7** claim. The car tested was an **MY20 ASX ES**, 2.0 litre with CVT, $26,740. The test route included "runs down a drag strip and 'spirited driving' on country roads". It is the same 110 kW / 197 Nm 2.0 and CVT as the 2021 LS, but it is not a test of a 2021 LS.
- **Official 2021 LS figure:** 7.6 L/100km (CarExpert specs).
- **Gentler real-world figures:** CarExpert's 2020 ASX LS review says it "settled well into the eights" over a week. The AAA real-world test (government programme, 93 km Geelong loop) gave the ASX ES 2.0 **8.2** vs 7.6 ADR.
- **Why it's common in WA:** WhichCar's March 2020 megatest says the ASX's sales lead over the second-placed CX-3 was "wider than ever" a decade after launch. It is a rental and fleet favourite (CarExpert calls it "a fleet favourite").
- **⚠ Price:** a 2021 ASX in Perth is advertised at about **$18–23k**, below the $25–30k band. 2023–2025 ASXs are the ones that sit at $22–26k. Either accept that Dave's car is a bit cheaper (which fits "the sensible buy that costs you at the pump") or move Dave to a 2023–24 ASX LS/MR (~$22–26k). Its official figure is still 7.6 (same engine).

### 3.2 Kia Seltos Sport 2.0 (2022)
- CarExpert specs: Sport 2.0 FWD **6.8** L/100km combined, 50 L tank.
- CarExpert 2022 Seltos S review: claimed 6.9 (S grade), "at test I got around the 7.2L/100km mark in mixed highway and city driving".
- WhichCar 2020 megatest: Seltos S 2.0 **8.0** tested, best of 10.
- Dash: Carsales' 2022 review describes a 4.2-inch digital display between analogue dials. Sources conflict on the Sport grade, so confirm with a photo of the actual car before the AI shot.

### 3.3 Mazda CX-3 G20 (2024)
- CarExpert 2024 CX-3 review: "uses 6.3L/100km on the combined cycle, we saw 7.3 litres per 100km on a week skewed to city driving". 48 L tank, 91 RON.
- WhichCar 2020 megatest: Maxx Sport 2.0 **8.7** tested. It was the only car in the test with stop/start.
- Dash (CarExpert G20 Touring SP review): a large analogue rev counter in the centre with a small digital speedometer readout at the bottom left, no temperature gauge, and a flip-up plastic head-up display.

### 3.4 Hyundai Kona 2.0 (2024, second generation)
- CarExpert 2024 Kona 2.0 review (19 Sep 2023): claimed **6.6** L/100km. "my driving loop returning an average of 7.3 litres every 100km".
- Dash: "basic digital instrument cluster shows speed, the rev counter and trip data, and changes colours" with the drive mode. The 12.3-inch cluster is only on higher grades.
- Note: a 2023 Kona could be either the old or the new shape. Use a 2024 or later car so the dash and the figures match.

### 3.5 Our 2018 Toyota C-HR Hybrid
- **The RACV 5.2 is verified, but it is a 2020 car.** RACV's "Toyota C-HR Hybrid 2020 road test review" (Tim Nicholson, 25 Mar 2020) says the official figure "is 4.3 litres per 100 kilometres and after three weeks of mixed country and city driving, we recorded a respectable 5.2L/100km". RACV didn't test a 2018 Hybrid. The Hybrid wasn't sold new in Australia until about 2020, so a 2018 Hybrid here is a Japanese import. Same 1.8 hybrid system.
- **Japanese-market figure:** JC08 **30.2 km/L** (model code DAA-ZYX10, 1.797 L, CVT), from the Japanese transport ministry (MLIT) fuel-economy list and Japanese press. 30.2 km/L = 100 / 30.2 = **3.3 L/100km**. JC08 is much gentler than ADR, so don't put it beside the ADR figures for the other cars. One Japanese real-world city test (Oct 2017) got 28.7 km/L ≈ 3.5 L/100km. That is a secondary source, not one of the approved Australian outlets.
- **Dash:** a JDM C-HR trip computer reads **km/L**. For our edit label or AI shot showing L/100km, either switch the unit in the car's settings and film it, or put the label in the edit, not on the dash.
